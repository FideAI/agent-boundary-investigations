## Corrections incorporated before this release

- Output detection checks two quoted field markers in a potentially truncated string; it does not require complete JSON.
- A submitted program is kept separate from a recorded response, script observation and downstream request. The seven-carrier audit documents two response mismatches.
- The 19:52 record does not corroborate a target fetch in the acquired JSON. This does not establish that the original researcher was wrong or that no other retained view supports it.
- The transfer restriction lookup uses trusted fixture labels. It does not track arbitrary data provenance through real transformations.
- Two fixed permission values do not implement mutable revocation.
- Complete controls share the same policy function. Their difference is service placement and an injected outage, not independently designed defense quality.
- The 47 route variants include 33 distinct operational configurations. Neither count is a sample of independent incidents.
- The recipient journal establishes bytes read, not durable storage. “Unknown delivery” is a limit of visibility, not an additional experimental condition.

## Checks and their limits

`verify_release.py` checks file hashes, source-pointer coverage and derived-data consistency. `reproduce_transfer.py` reruns qualification, the HTTP comparison and challenges in a temporary copy. Archive analyses require the separately acquired pinned source JSON. Source availability and mutable archive metadata can prevent exact reacquisition.

These checks do not authenticate a historical actor, establish complete archive coverage or show improved autonomous defensive decisions. A source-linked reporting record is a proposed aid; no intervention effectiveness claim is made.

The public claims and notes expose our reasoning for challenge. Substantive post-release corrections belong in `CHANGELOG.md` with their effect on conclusions.
